Overview#

Creative North Star: "The Control Desk"

Porto is one dense, professional control desk for local apps, containers, images, builds, storage, networks, Kubernetes, and VMs. It uses a fixed instrument rail, fused fleet signal and control strips, ranked inventories, and a docked inspector. The palette is neutral graphite with three operational signal colors: olive, amber, and fault red. Porto deliberately avoids the identity accents of other desktop container tools; no pink, purple, or bright brand blue appears in the interface.

The story order is deliberate: choose a workload family from the rail, read its fleet health, find the affected resource in the ranked inventory, act through visible controls, then inspect routing, runtime details, logs, terminals, files, metrics, or maintenance without losing the list.

Key characteristics:

  • Dark theme by default; a light theme and "Match system" are available in Settings > Theme.
  • Neutral graphite (dark) or cool off-white (light) working surfaces. No warm beige, putty, or paper tones.
  • Instrument surfaces (navigation rail, fleet signal rail, drawer readouts, command strip, log console, terminals) stay dark in both themes.
  • Status meaning is carried by exactly three signal colors.
  • Monospace, uppercase, letter-spaced labels for machine-reported values.
  • Hairline borders separate regions; shadows are soft and subtle.

Themes#

The theme is stored with the other experience preferences (theme: dark, light, or system; default dark). public/experience-preferences.js applies html[data-theme] before React renders so there is no flash, and the preferences provider keeps it in sync, following prefers-color-scheme when the theme is system. Every color in App.css is a token from index.css; theme-specific tokens live under :root[data-theme="dark"] and :root[data-theme="light"], while instrument tokens are shared.

Colors#

Signals (both themes)#

  • Olive (dark #8fa04f / light #64703a): healthy, running, ready.
  • Amber (dark #d58d33 / light #bd7725): in progress, attention, and the universal keyboard focus outline.
  • Fault red (dark #cf5a4c / light #b0443a): crashed, error, destructive.

Each signal has -soft (tinted background) and -text (readable foreground) variants per theme.

Neutrals#

  • Canvas: dark #141516, light #f3f4f5.
  • Panel / raised / hover / selected: dark #1b1c1e / #222326 / #27292c / #2c2e32; light #ffffff / #ffffff / #f4f5f6 / #eceef0.
  • Fields: dark #101112, light #ffffff.
  • Lines: dark #2b2d31 / #3b3e43; light #e1e3e6 / #c7cacf.
  • Ink: dark #e7e8ea / #a6a9ae / #7b7f86; light #17181a / #4d5057 / #71757c.
  • Instrument: #0f1011 rail and readouts, #0c0d0e consoles.

Named rules#

The Three-Signal Rule. Only olive, amber, and fault red carry operational meaning. Links use a desaturated slate (--link) so they never compete with signals.

The Neutral Rule. Working surfaces stay neutral gray. Never introduce warm beige or a brand-identity hue as a surface or accent color.

Typography#

UI font: the platform system stack (-apple-system, SF Pro, Segoe UI, Helvetica Neue, Inter, Roboto). Label/mono font: ui-monospace, SF Mono, Menlo, Consolas.

Hierarchy#

  • Display (700, clamp(1.45rem, 2vw, 1.8rem)): page h1 only.
  • Headline (600, 17px): panel and settings section headings.
  • Body (400, 14px base; 12–13px in dense rows).
  • Label (mono, 10–12px, uppercase, 0.05em): machine-reported values such as state, ports, PIDs, branches, timestamps, and commands.

Layout#

The shell uses a fixed 240px instrument rail and a fluid main area padded 22px 32px 48px. A resource screen is a single column until something is selected; then .workArea:has(> .inspector) adds an inspector column of --inspector-width (clamp(400px, 32vw, 560px)), so creation and detail panels such as New cluster have room for their forms. The fleet signal rail and control bar are fused into one instrument. Control bars wrap their actions instead of overflowing.

Inventories are joined tables: one bordered container whose rows are separated by hairlines, highlight on hover, and mark the selected row with a tinted background and a 2px inset bar.

Responsive behavior:

  • ≤1080px: inventory columns compress; the inspector narrows to min(400px, 44vw).
  • ≤860px: the rail becomes an off-canvas panel over a scrim, inspectors become full-screen detail surfaces, and the work area is one column.
  • ≤620px: control bar items take the full width; secondary machine facts hide and touch targets grow.

Elevation and shape#

Surfaces are separated by 1px borders, not shadows. Resting buttons use --shadow-sm; menus, the expanded channel, and the maximized inspector use --shadow-md; tooltips and popovers use --shadow-pop. Hard offset "printed" shadows are not used.

Radii are 4px (--radius-sm: chips, small controls), 6px (--radius-md: buttons, inputs, rows), and 8px (--radius-lg: panels, control bar, inspector). Status lamps and the brand-mark dots are the only circles.

Components#

Buttons#

13px/500 sans, 6px radius, --line-strong border on --panel-raised. Hover raises to --panel-hover with --line-hover; active uses --panel-selected. Focus-visible is a 3px amber outline with a 2px offset. Disabled is 0.48 opacity. Icon buttons keep their role colors (olive, amber, red, slate), a visible tooltip, an aria-label, and visually hidden text.

Inputs#

--field background, --line-strong border, 6px radius. Focus darkens the border and adds the olive underline glow.

Environment controls#

The global environment selector shares the settings field styles and lives in a bordered panel separated from page content by 20px. Identity and connection facts use compact text, with the target ID in monospace. Manage environments uses the standard button-link treatment rather than a browser-default link. Warnings retain their own full-width rows.

Environment management uses a joined, bordered inventory with top-aligned details and wrapping actions. Expanded registration, listener, metadata, and fleet panels pair explanatory text with consistently spaced forms; they stack below 860px. Capability checkboxes stay beside their sentence-case labels. Wide environment tables scroll within their panel rather than widening the page.

Software updates#

Software updates is the first Settings panel, with a visible Check for updates action and an inline status readout. It uses the same two-column section layout and three-signal status treatment as the other settings panels. Desktop startup and automatic-download preferences remain in Desktop behavior.

Navigation rail#

Dark in every theme. Four main sections, Development, Containers, Kubernetes, and System, replace the long flat inventory of links. Containers and Kubernetes use short, task-oriented subgroups; every destination still opens its own existing page, not a combined screen or extra tab strip. Nested links use indentation instead of repeating resource icons.

Main sections and subgroups expand independently. Collapsing a main section preserves its subgroup choices. Expand all and Collapse all affect every available section and subgroup. Opening a page, following a bookmark, or using browser history automatically reveals its branch without closing other sections. Native-button toggles use chevrons, aria-controls, and aria-expanded; the current page keeps aria-current, a subtle light fill, and a neutral inset marker. See the complete navigation map.

At 860px or below, the closed rail is inert and hidden. Opening it preserves expansion choices and focuses the current page link, or the Porto brand link if that branch is collapsed. It traps keyboard focus within navigation and its close button, and blocks interaction with the underlying content. Escape, the scrim, or choosing a page closes it and restores focus to the trigger. Navigation controls have 44px touch targets. A Skip to content link bypasses the rail without changing the current route.

Status lamp#

A 9px LED-style circle: olive running, amber starting, red crashed or error, gray stopped. It is always paired with a text state.

Integration status#

Provider and tool readiness rows show a 2px inset left bar in the signal color (olive ready, amber working or missing, red error).

Do's and don'ts#

Do#

  • Keep dark as the default theme and every color tokenized.
  • Keep instrument surfaces dark in both themes.
  • Reserve olive, amber, and fault red for health, progress, and fault.
  • Keep the inventory visible while the inspector is open.
  • Give every icon-only control an accessible name and a tooltip.

Don't#

  • Use warm beige, putty, or paper backgrounds, or a ruled background texture.
  • Use pink, purple, or bright brand blue accents.
  • Use hard offset shadows or radii above 8px.
  • Add decorative or looping motion; reveal transitions stay around 150–220ms and are disabled under reduced motion.
  • Hide state behind an icon or a color alone.
